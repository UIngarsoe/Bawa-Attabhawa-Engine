Abstract

Classroom 72 examines how ordinary civilian questions can function as an intelligence-delivery technology when transformed into structured analytical problems.

The FM component is deliberately a simulation. The researcher does not claim to have conducted an actual radio broadcast, collected authentic telephone testimony, or interviewed the simulated callers.

Instead, the human contribution is the design of civilian real-world questions concerning:

1. price differences between Yangon and Naypyidaw;
2. rumours concerning new-city or capital relocation;
3. the relationship between elite-linked development projects and civilian uncertainty; and
4. foreign investment under political, legal, sanctions, and reputational risk.

AI is then used to generate the FM host, caller dialogue, explanatory transitions, and educational simulation.

This distinction is methodologically central:

«The AI generates the simulation.
The researcher designs the intelligence question.
Evidence determines the conclusion.»

The classroom therefore demonstrates a model of civil intelligence education rather than intelligence collection.

---

1. Research Question

The central question of Classroom 72 is:

«How can ordinary civilian questions be transformed into disciplined analytical intelligence without allowing the simulation itself to become false evidence?»

The classroom tests three examples:

Question A — Economic

Why might the same consumer product cost substantially more in Naypyidaw than Yangon?

Question B — Institutional

Does development around Pyin Oo Lwin / Yadanabon New City demonstrate an intention to abandon or replace Naypyidaw?

Question C — Investment

How should a foreign investor evaluate long-horizon projects under conditions of political instability, sanctions exposure, legal uncertainty, and reputational risk?

---

2. Methodological Innovation

2.1 Civilian Question as Intelligence-Delivery Technology

The key innovation of Classroom 72 is not the simulated radio host.

It is the question architecture.

A civilian asks:

«“Why does this cost twice as much?”»

SSISM asks:

«What observable variables could produce the difference?»

A civilian asks:

«“Are they building another capital?”»

SSISM asks:

«What evidence would distinguish a development project from institutional relocation?»

A civilian asks:

«“Would foreigners invest?”»

SSISM asks:

«Which investors, under which jurisdiction, with what sanctions exposure, financing channel, ownership structure, and time horizon?»

[
